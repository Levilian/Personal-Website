Building Raycaster

I’m Levi. I work on AI for documents that have to be right — the files that decide whether a drug, a contract, or a plant gets approved.

I grew up in a four-generation medical family and was supposed to become a doctor. At Stanford I studied Symbolic Systems instead. I wanted to accelerate the science more than I wanted to practice it.

The spark for Raycaster was watching friends with PhDs spend their weeks reconciling Word files. The science was fine. The documents were the bottleneck. We build an AI workspace for that work: cited findings, human review, and certainty you can defend.

Before this I founded Yona, a companion for Gen Z mental wellness, and shipped product at Uber and Intuit. I still think a lot about evaluation — whether an agent can actually do the work, not just score well on a benchmark.

Some things I believe

  • In this kind of work, the document is the product.
  • Workflows first, chat last.
  • A leaderboard score is not a finished artifact.
  • Sit with the files. That’s where the work is.
  • Depth in one vertical beats a chatbot that can “do anything.”
  • The years after discovery are mostly coordination.
  • Evaluation is how you know — and how you improve.
  • Trust is earned by putting something useful in someone’s hands, then iterating.
  • It’s easier to do a hard specific thing than a vague important one.
